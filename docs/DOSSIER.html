Part I · The research

What the contest rewards, and who else is in it

Six weeks of reading condensed into four pages: the rules that decide everything, the field that sets the bar, the pain that justifies the product, and the idea those three produced.

1 · The contest as it is actually judged

Two stages. Stage 1 is pass/fail and asks only two things: does the project fit the theme, and does it reasonably apply the required APIs. A beautiful project with a decorative PayPal button dies here and is never scored.

Stage 2 scores five criteria, equally weighted. The tiebreak goes to the first one listed, which is Technological Implementation — so in a field this size, where scores cluster, depth beats polish when the two compete for the same hour.

3 · The pain, sourced

The product thesis is narrow: the decision to offer a buy-now-pay-later plan is made without any model of whether this person can carry it. The harm numbers are not about the product existing. They are about it being offered indiscriminately, in small repeated badly-timed payments.

FindingFigureSource
BNPL users who paid late at least once in the past year47%LendingTree, 2026 — up from 41% (2025) and 34% (2024)
BNPL users holding multiple simultaneous loans63%CFPB, January 2025
…of whom, across multiple lenders33%CFPB, January 2025
BNPL complaints, 2026 to date10,884CFPB complaint database — against 7,412 in all of 2025, +47% with two months to run

The caveat that belongs in the pitch, not the footnotes

The 47% is self-reported. The CFPB's dollar-weighted charge-off rate stays under 2%. Both are true and they measure different things. Saying "47% of BNPL users default" would be wrong, and a judge may know it. The correct sentence: "47% report paying late at least once, while dollar-weighted losses stay under 2% — the harm is concentrated in small, repeated, badly-timed payments, which is exactly the pattern a per-purchase check could prevent."

4 · The idea and its rules

Second Thought. At the moment someone is about to split a payment into four, ask a question nobody else asks: is this good for this person? Project their next 45 days, then answer with one of three things.

AnswerRuleWhat actually happens
OFFERevery one of the four payments leaves the balance at or above the floor, every day for 45 daysPayPal AUTHORIZE hold, captured on the approved date
DEFERthat fails, but a single payment dated to the next payday passes, and the payday is inside the 21-day deferral windowAn Invoicing invoice due on payday. The merchant ships when it clears
DECLINEneither works, including the partial ladderNothing offered. The collision is named and the largest amount that would work is quoted

The arithmetic, which is deliberately boring

  • Horizon: 45 days. Long enough to catch next month's rent, short enough to stay honest.
  • Floor: max(10% of monthly income, $50). Not a credit score, not a limit — a "don't let this week break you" line.
  • Instalments: four, fourteen days apart.
  • Deferral window: 21 days. Beyond that it is not a deferral, it is a different month.
  • Determinism: the same calendar and the same amount always produce the same answer. There is no sampling, no temperature, no model in this path.
  • Partial ladder: 90/75/60/50/40/25/10% now, remainder on payday — a fixed ladder, because feasibility is not monotonic in the split and a binary search would be unsound.

Why this is not another BNPL app

DEFER is pay-before-ship. The merchant extends no credit and funds no receivable; the shopper never borrows; the goods move when the money arrives. Compare a card decline, where a good customer is simply lost. Here the sale moves to a date the shopper can actually meet. The trade is timing, not volume — and it is stated that way, because "volume preserved" is a claim we cannot support.

What could make this derivative, and the escape route

RiskMitigation, applied in the build
"Responsible lending" reads as a lectureThe refusal carries a number: "Up to $140.00 would work today." Show the maths and the option, never "you can't afford this"
A judge asks why PayPal would reduce its own BNPL volumeBecause a denied-then-explained shopper returns and a silently-blacklisted one churns — and because the alternative to a deferral is a lost sale, not a full-price one
Looks like a budgeting appIt has no dashboard, no categories, no goals. It has one question and three answers, inside a checkout
Part II · The build

What was constructed, and where each guarantee lives

Seven and a half thousand lines of Python, no third-party packages, every file under 300 lines. The layering is not decoration: it is what makes the AI boundary and the security model checkable by a person in ten minutes.

6 · The PayPal integration

Every call is server-side. No client secret reaches the browser, and the live adapter refuses to run if PAYPAL_ENV is anything but sandbox.

PayPal surfaceEndpointUsed for
OAuth 2 client credentialsPOST /v1/oauth2/tokenevery call below; token cached for its 9-hour life
Orders v2 — create + AUTHORIZEPOST /v2/checkout/ordersan OFFER reserves the funds rather than charging them
CapturePOST /v2/payments/authorizations/{id}/captureon the approved date
VoidPOST /v2/payments/authorizations/{id}/voidrelease when the shopper changes their mind
Invoicing v2POST /v2/invoicing/invoicesthe DEFER invoice, payment_term.due_date set by the engine's payday maths
Webhooks/api/webhooksignature-verified; INVOICING.INVOICE.PAID is what turns a deferral into a shipment
IdempotencyPayPal-Request-Ida retried call returns the first result instead of creating a second charge

Webhook verification, in full

The signed string is transmissionId|transmissionTime|webhookId|crc32(rawBody) computed over the raw bytes, checked with RSA-SHA256 PKCS#1 v1.5 against a certificate fetched from a URL inside the event. The certificate URL must be HTTPS and on a PayPal host, and that check lives in the key-fetch path, not only in the fetch helper — a security check in one code path is not a check. The implementation uses only the standard library: no cryptography, no requests.

7 · The AI boundary

The model's only job is reading a human sentence into structured facts. It cannot decide anything, and that is enforced structurally rather than promised in a README.

Failure modeWhat the system does
Low confidence on a fieldThe field is kept, capped in confidence, and flagged to the shopper with a question. It is not silently trusted.
Nothing readableThe answer degrades to partial or demo. It never guesses and never crashes.
Extraction graded demoprofile_from raises. The engine never sees it.
Junk wording, valid factsThe verdict does not move. There is a test for exactly this.
No key configuredA deterministic template reader runs instead, and the on-screen badge says AI · LLM-MOCK.

Why the boring choice is the good one

Most submissions put the model in charge and hope. Here the model is a very good parser and nothing else: temperature: 0, JSON mode, per-field confidence, and an agent that raises on anything it cannot stand behind. It is the least fashionable use of an LLM in this competition and the only one we can defend line by line.

8 · The security model

ControlImplementation
Session isolationA session per cookie, in memory, with its own signing secret. A token minted in one visitor's gate cannot verify in another's — this was a real defect, found and fixed.
Human approvalHMAC over the tool name and the exact arguments, single use, 600-second TTL. Nothing reaches PayPal until it is redeemed.
Tamper refusalAn edited amount, an altered due date, a replayed token, an expired token and a foreign token are all refused with 403 before the gateway is called.
Rate limitsTwo-key: 30 model calls per session and 90 per IP per 600 s, so clearing a cookie does not buy a fresh budget.
Spend capIn-process daily call budget (1,500) as a backstop; the provider-side cap is the real protection and is set in the account.
Health endpoint/healthz returns four booleans and a status word. No keys, no paths, no versions.
Diagnostics/diagnostics returns 404 unless a token is configured, and counts only when it is.

9 · The decision engine

The core is a projection over a horizon, run once per candidate schedule. Its honesty comes from what it refuses to do: it will not spread a balance thinly and call it affordable, and it will not offer an instrument whose date is already in the past.

10 · The screen

One page. Three months visible without a click, each with its amount. The answer arrives as a sentence, not a status code, and the internal vocabulary never reaches the shopper — a test fails the build if words like verdict, collision or token appear in a sentence a shopper reads.

BadgesAI · LLM / AI · LLM-MOCK and PAYMENTS · SANDBOX / PAYMENTS · SIMULATED, computed from configuration and never overridable by argument
AssetsInline CSS, inline SVG favicon, no external fonts, no CDN, no analytics — it renders on a locked-down laptop with no network
Empty state"Pick one of the three and the answer appears here" — the page is never a blank void

12 · Five things that broke

1 · The demo broke by itself, and that was the best bug

The three demo months were pinned to November 2026 dates, and /api/plan passed a hardcoded date(2026, 11, 1). Nothing in the code changed; the calendar moved. Ben's payday passed, the reservation refused a date in the past, and the DEFER path — the hero of the pitch — returned HTTP 500. Judging runs 1–15 December, when every date would be stale and a demo the rules require to be runnable would not run.

Fix: months generated relative to today; a test walks sixty days forward asserting all three verdicts still appear; a lint fails the build if a literal date re-enters the demo path.

2 · The .env nothing read

.env.example said "copy to .env and fill in", and only the precondition checker had a reader. Anyone following the instructions would set up credentials and then be told they were missing — while looking straight at them.

Fix: config.py loads product/.env at import, real environment variables win, and ST_ENV=production ignores the file entirely so a stray .env can never override a dashboard.

3 · pay_on was populated for OFFER

The recommended option for an OFFER carries an invoice_due — the first instalment — and the quote copied it into pay_on. The screen would have told a shopper who can pay today to wait.

Fix: the field is populated only for DEFER, with a test asserting it.

4 · Dollars against minor units

A hundred-fold error waiting to happen. Every amount in the system is an integer of cents, and there is exactly one place that converts. The other half was payday arithmetic: a fortnightly cadence with no anchor date must not borrow today's date at high confidence, so it caps the confidence and asks a question instead.

5 · A security check in one code path

The webhook certificate URL was validated in the fetch helper and not in the key path, so a second caller could reach an unvalidated URL. One check in one place is not a check.

Fix: validation moved into _key(), where every caller passes through.

Part V · The honest parts

What is missing, what would lose, and what happens next

18 · Limits, stated plainly

LimitWhat it costs, and what we say instead
No live PayPal call in this workspaceStage 1 asks whether PayPal is genuinely applied. The adapter is written and tested against a protocol-identical simulation; it has not moved sandbox money here. On your machine it does — and the artifacts go in the appendix.
No users (n = 0)The claim is "researched, built and tested by us". Never "validated". Five conversations are scheduled; if they do not happen, the pitch says so early rather than being found out late.
No stranger has tried the screen (V8, n = 0)We wrote it to be understood and have not watched anyone try. The plain-language audit is a proxy, and it caught two real defects — it is not the same as comprehension.
No deploymentThree gates and the video stand on a public URL. Until it exists, every Design claim is unverifiable by anyone.
Session state is in memoryA redeploy logs everyone out. Correct for a demo, and said out loud rather than discovered by a judge.
Pay Later cannot be originated in sandboxEligibility is decided client-side from amount and country. So the mechanic sits on AUTHORIZE and Invoicing, which we control end to end — a stronger promise than borrowing, and honestly labelled as a different primitive.

Three ways this loses anyway

  1. The safe path gets taken. A lookalike "AI shopping agent with a budget" sits in a cluster of 47. Anything that reads as responsible-lending advice is scored as a lecture. The refusal must be shown as a feature with a number in it.
  2. The claim outruns the evidence. "Validated with users" at n = 0 is the fastest way to lose a judge who checks. The honesty costs nothing and buys the rest of the document.
  3. It is not there in December. The rules require availability until 15 Dec. Memory sessions, a sleeping free tier, or a stale demo data set each break that — the date bug in §12 was exactly this failure, and was found by luck rather than by design.

The V8 rehearsal, as a script

Ninety seconds, one person who has never seen the screen, no explanation from you. Hand over a phone on /checkout?persona=ada and say nothing. Then ask these five questions in order and write down their answers verbatim:

#AskPass looks like
1Read the first screen out loud.No question about what the product is; no request for context.
2Pick “defer”. What did you just agree to?“Half now, half in a fortnight” without opening any help text.
3You want to pay early. Where do you go?Finds the change control in under twenty seconds.
4Whose money is this, and is it a loan?Says the shop’s money, through PayPal; says it is not credit.
5Change your mind and go back. Dead end?Returns cleanly; the badge still tells the truth about what is paid.

The first sentence out of their mouth containing “wait” or “so…” is the copy you rewrite that night — not a new feature. n = 1 beats n = 0 by enough that it is worth the hour.
