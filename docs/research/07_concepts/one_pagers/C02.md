## 1. The sentence

**"Every other BNPL tool asks how to sell more with instalments. This one decides whether an instalment is actually good for this person — and tells them the truth, including 'no'."**

## 2. Who hurts, and how much

**Audience:** two sides, one product.
- **The buyer** using Pay Later who cannot see their total exposure and gets silently denied or silently stacked.
- **The merchant** whose BNPL conversions turn into disputes, chargebacks and locked-out repeat customers.

The evidence is unusually strong for this space:

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| BNPL users who paid late in the past year | **47%** (34% → 41% → 47% across 2024–26) | LendingTree 2026 |
| Say BNPL caused them to overspend | **68%** | LendingTree |
| Regret using it | **54%** | LendingTree |
| Hold multiple simultaneous BNPL loans | **63%** (33% across multiple lenders) | CFPB Jan 2025 |
| Triggered an overdraft or NSF fee | **11%** | CFPB |
| Avg late fee, PayPal Pay Later | **$9.99**, charged to **4.1%** of loans | CFPB Dec 2025 |
| BNPL market size | **~$160B** | industry |

**State this honestly in the submission:** charge-off is only **1.83% (2023)**. Self-reported late payment and formal charge-off measure different things. The harm is not catastrophic default — it is **small, repeated, badly-timed payments that cascade into fees and lockouts.**

Ledger entries: **P07–P13, P23**. Evidence strength: **strong** (two independent national surveys plus a CFPB report naming PayPal).

## 3. What the user does

> **Superseded:** steps 2, 3 and 5 below describe mechanics PayPal's APIs do not allow
> (cross-lender plan data; merchant-altered instalment schedules). The validated flow is in
> `C02_PRD.md` §3 and §7, and implemented in `../../../../09_build/prototype/.env.example`.

1. At checkout, Pay Later would be offered as usual.
2. The **affordability agent** assembles what it can actually see: every plan *we* originated for this shopper, their declared obligations, and their pay dates.
3. It returns one of three verdicts in words the shopper can read: **offer it**, **invoice it to payday**, or **"not this month, and here is which bill it collides with."**
4. If the user proceeds, they get a **repayment calendar**: every instalment, every payday, every essential bill, and the projected balance after each.
5. If a payment is about to land badly, it **routes the transaction to a different instrument** — an invoice dated to the payday — instead of letting a late fee happen and then getting the shopper blacklisted.
6. On denial, it quotes a **safe amount** ("up to $200 works") so the refusal becomes a smaller sale rather than a lost one.

**The demo moment:** the agent declines to offer Pay Later to a user who could technically take it — because the calendar shows it would collide with two other instalments and a rent date. That is a screen no other entrant is building.

## 4. What the agent decides vs. what code decides

| Agent | Deterministic code |
|---|---|
| Reads the situation, explains the recommendation in plain language | The **affordability arithmetic** in integer cents: income dates, committed outflows, existing instalments, buffer |
| Generates the alternative schedule and the user-facing rationale | **Eligibility and limits** never come from the model — they come from the API's own response |
| Writes the notification and calendar annotations | The **decision to offer/decline** is a rules engine over deterministic inputs; the model can only phrase it |

**Do not build a model that approves credit.** Build a model that explains and schedules. This distinction is what makes the project defensible to a PayPal judge who knows the regulatory exposure (ledger P13).

## 6. Why now

From the Q3 2026 call: **"More than 20% of 2026 transaction margin dollars come from financial services, now growing double digits."** BNPL is a named growth engine, and **PayPal funds the merchant upfront, so it carries the credit risk on every instalment it writes** (ledger P13). That means PayPal has the most to lose from a badly-timed instalment and the most to gain from getting it right. *(1 Nov correction: the earlier version claimed a credit-bureau reporting asymmetry. Affirm has reported Pay in 4 to Experian since Apr 2025 and TransUnion since May 2025; the claim was removed.)*

The CFPB's Dec 2025 report evaluated PayPal by name. Regulatory attention plus a growth mandate plus a real harm profile is the exact situation where a "responsible by design" product wins favour internally.

## 7. Why this is not already covered

**`credit-bnpl` is the third-thinnest cluster in the field: 3 repos out of 549.**

Critically, **none of the top-depth repos in the field scan touch it.** The 12 strongest entries are all mandate gates, dispute evidence, escrow, and storefront agents. The consumer-side BNPL decision is wide open.

And the qualitative evidence in `../../04_pain/QUALITATIVE_QUOTES.md` sections D–E shows the specific unserved pain: **silent automated denial, no reason given, no human override, option vanishes for months or years after a single late payment.** Nobody is building for that user.

## 10. What could make this derivative — and the mitigation

**Risks:**
- The sandbox cannot reliably offer a real Pay Later plan (see `../../05_capability/SANDBOX_AND_GOTCHAS.md` §8). **Do not make a live Pay Later offer the central moment.** Make the mechanic something you control: the affordability engine, the AUTHORIZE hold, the calendar, the rescheduling. Say clearly in the README what is live and what is simulated in sandbox.
- "Responsible lending" products can read as a lecture. **The mitigation is the honest, specific refusal with a better alternative** — not moralising, and never "you can't afford it." Show the maths and the option.
- A judge may ask "why would PayPal want to reduce its own BNPL volume?" **Have the answer ready:** because PayPal carries the credit-reporting exposure (P13), because a denied-then-explained user returns and a silently-blacklisted one churns, and because the CEO said financial services is the growth engine — not the volume engine.

**This is the recommendation.** It has the best evidence, the highest intent fit, the thinnest cluster among the strong concepts, no derivative risk, a natural AG Grid surface, and a demo moment nobody else has.
