# C06 — Goal-Bound Remittance

*One-pager · EV #4 (48.6) · intent fit 3/5 · 1 competitor · STRONG impact, weakest strategic fit*

---

## 1. The sentence

**"Money sent home buys something specific. This agent lets the sender pay for that thing — school fees, medicine, a roof — instead of sending cash and hoping."**

## 2. Who hurts, and how much

**Audience:** migrant workers sending money to family in India (and the wider $685B LMIC inflow market), and the recipients who manage several senders' money at once.

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Global average cost to send $200 | **6.36%** ($12.72) | World Bank RPW Issue 54, Q3 2025 |
| Digital average | **4.59%** | same |
| Bank average | **14.99%** | same |
| Cheapest three operators (SmaRT) | **3.29%** | same |
| **India corridor average** | **5.30%** | same |
| UN SDG 10.c target | **<3% by 2030** | UN |
| India inflows, FY2025-26 | **$144.8B** net private transfers, **+16%** | RBI |
| Largest senders to India | **US 27.7%, UAE 19.2%** | World Bank |
| Loss per sender at the average-vs-best gap | **≈$147/year** on $400/month | derived |

The sharp line, from `../../04_pain/EVIDENCE_NOTES.md` §5: **the good price already exists — it just isn't delivered.** A sender paying 5.30% when 3.29% is available is losing money to search costs and habit, not to the market.

Ledger entries: **P14–P17**. Evidence strength: **very strong** (World Bank + central bank data, not surveys).

## 3. What the user does

1. The **recipient** sends a voice note in their own language ("the school wants ₹18,000 by the 5th, and Amma's medicine is ₹2,400").
2. The agent **structures the need** into itemised goals with amounts, payees and deadlines.
3. The **sender** reviews and approves — seeing the total, the fee, and the effective rate, with the cheapest available route shown alongside.
4. The payment **splits**: a direct bill payment to the school, a payment to the pharmacy, and the remainder as cash.
5. **Proof comes back** — a receipt, a confirmation. The sender sees what their money bought.
6. A **savings goal** carries the surplus forward instead of it dissolving into the household budget.

## 4. What the agent decides vs. what code decides

| Agent | Deterministic code |
|---|---|
| Parses the voice note / message into candidate goals (multilingual, ambiguous input — the right job for a model) | Amounts, splits, FX conversion, and fee arithmetic — in integer minor units, never floats |
| Matches a goal to a known biller or payee | Route selection from actual quoted rates; the "cheapest" claim is computed, not asserted |
| Writes the confirmation the sender receives | Payment execution only on explicit human approval of a computed total |
| Explains the fee breakdown | **The model never chooses the amount or the route.** |

## 5. PayPal surface

| API / tool | Why it is the right primitive |
|---|---|
| **Payouts** (`/v1/payments/payouts`) | Multi-recipient, multi-currency disbursement — batch splits to several payees |
| **Orders v2** | Funding the sender's side |
| **Multi-currency + FX** | Only 3% of the field touches cross-border pricing; it is the core of this concept |
| **Invoicing** | Structured bill requests to a school or clinic — 0.5% field usage |
| **Webhooks + verification** | Delivery and settlement state |
| **AG Grid Studio** | The sender's lifetime transfer ledger and goal tracker |

## 6. Why now

Weaker than the other two, and say so. PayPal's own narrative is **agentic commerce**, not remittance — although **Xoom is explicitly in scope** for this hackathon, which makes a remittance build legitimate.

The genuine "why now" is the SDG 10.c deadline pressure: the world committed to <3% by 2030, the India corridor sits at 5.30%, and digital rails are at 4.59%. The gap is real, measured, and public.

## 7. Why this is not already covered

**`remittance-crossborder` is the thinnest cluster in the entire field: 1 repo out of 549.**

But read the caveat: it is thin partly because it is *hard*, and partly because it is **not on PayPal's developer narrative**. A thin cluster that judges find off-theme is not an advantage. This concept wins on Impact and loses on Innovation-adjacency.

## 10. What makes this risky — and the mitigation

**Risks:**
1. **Intent fit 3/5.** A PayPal judge scoring *Innovation* may ask "how does this advance agentic commerce?" Have the answer: the agent that *understands what the money is for* is the agent that can be trusted to spend it — the same trust problem PayPal's CEO described, applied to the highest-volume cross-border flow on earth.
2. **Sandbox realism.** Cross-border payouts and FX in sandbox are approximations. Be explicit about what is real. Do not fake a currency conversion curve.
3. **Feasibility.** Eleven days. If you take this, **cut scope hard**: one corridor (US → India), one language pair, three goal types, and no biller-directory integration beyond one worked example.

**Take this if** you are confident in multilingual voice handling and want the strongest human story in the contest. **Do not take this** if you want the safest path to a high score — C02 dominates it on feasibility and intent fit at the same judge_score.

**Honest ranking note:** this has the **highest raw judge_score in the library (4.8)** — but scoring 4.8 on paper means nothing if it is 70% built on 10 November. The EV formula's feasibility term exists precisely to price that in.
